=========================================

Fatal times and intervals:

    913.640795   sleepmgr:4054   (boot start ~0; the modem came up at ~12.9 s)
   1816.046634   sleepmgr:4054   interval 902.405839 s
   2718.436833   sleepmgr:4054   interval 902.390199 s
   3477.969305   a2_power.c:2949 interval 759.532472 s   <-- NOT the clock

The sleepmgr clock was running true (902.406, 902.390 -- 17 ppm apart) and would
have produced its 4th beat at 2718.436833 + 902.4 = 3620.83 s.  Instead an
a2_power.c:2949 event fired 142.87 s EARLY and pre-empted it.

This is NOT a new anomaly.  Doc 162's taxonomy already separates the signatures
by their period distribution -- common_timer n=11 at 902.353 s (+/-281 ppm, the
clock), sleepmgr n=10 at 901.230 s, and a2_power n=7 spanning 68.5-941.3 s which
is NOT a clock.  759.53 s sits inside the a2_power band.  So the correct reading
is the one Doc 164 established: two mechanisms with different periods compete
inside one boot, and here the a2_power one won the race.

The ledger's history agrees that a2_power.c:2949 is not clock-locked: it appears
at uptime 719.00 s and 896.52 s in two earlier boots (see Part 3).

WHY THE MECHANISM IS PLAUSIBLE (stated as a hypothesis, NOT adopted)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The resync branch, added by patch 808, does four things, two of which the MODEM
can see:

    WRITE_ONCE(dmux->pm_suspend_start_ns, 0);      /* AP-local */
    WRITE_ONCE(dmux->pc_state, true);              /* AP-local */
    bam_dmux_pm_restart(dmux);                     /* MODEM-VISIBLE: powers the
                                                      BAM down and up again */
    bam_dmux_pc_ack(dmux);                         /* MODEM-VISIBLE: the SMSM
                                                      power-collapse ACK */
  then complete_all(&dmux->pc_ack_completion);     /* AP-local */
       wake_up_all(&dmux->pc_wait);                /* AP-local */

  Reading A (the resync is a POISON PILL): the ACK and/or the BAM restart lands
  on a modem whose power state machine has already moved on, and a2_power
  asserts.  Under A the resync->fatal gap is a modem reaction constant, so it
  should NOT vary with how late the AP noticed the edge.

  Reading B (COMMON CAUSE): the modem's PC state machine had already
  desynchronised, which is why the edge was lost AND why a2_power asserts.  The
  resync is a symptom.

  Reading C (the AP's MISSED EDGE is the cause): the modem asserts the PC line
  and has a bounded wait for the ACK; the AP missed the edge and only the
  watchdog finds it, so the ACK arrives outside that window and the modem's
  timeout is what a2_power.c:2949 reports.  Under C the gap is
  (modem_timeout - edge_delay), so it SHOULD vary with the AP's detection delay.

  THE WATCHDOG POLLS EVERY 100 ms, so the AP's detection delay is uniform on
  [0, 100] ms (qcom_bam_dmux.c, patch 808:
  `schedule_delayed_work(&dmux->rx_watchdog_work, msecs_to_jiffies(100))`).
  The measured spread is 11.842 ms over 3 samples.  If Reading C were right the
  three edge delays would have had to land inside an 11.8 ms window out of 100 ms
  -- roughly a 4 % coincidence.  That mildly favours A over C, and is far too
  weak to act on.  It is recorded so the next session does not have to redo it.

  Reading A does NOT distinguish WHICH of the two modem-visible actions is the
  trigger, and the measurement cannot: both are inside the same 74-86 ms.

  NOT YET EXAMINED: `pc_timeout_count: 4` in rx_telemetry.  If that counter is
  the AP's own PC-handshake timeout it may be measuring the same window from the
  other side, and reading its definition is the cheapest next step.

THE DISCRIMINATOR, AND WHY IT IS CHEAP
--------------------------------------
qcom_bam_dmux IS A LOADABLE MODULE on this device:

    # lsmod | grep bam
    qcom_bam_dmux          32768  0
    # ls -la /lib/modules/6.12.94/qcom_bam_dmux.ko
    -rw-r--r--  1 root root 241232 Sep 21 08:07 .../qcom_bam_dmux.ko

so a variant that changes ONLY the resync branch can be deployed by swapping the
.ko -- no kernel flash, exactly the patch-823 playbook (scratch/mkko823.sh).

Two designs, in increasing strength:

  D1 (observational, zero behaviour change).  Add a dev_warn immediately before
     bam_dmux_pc_ack() and immediately after bam_dmux_pm_restart(), so the fatal
     can be timed against each action separately rather than against the single
     resync line.  Cost: one build.  Answers nothing on its own -- the two
     actions are < 1 ms apart -- but it removes the assumption.

  D2 (behavioural, decisive).  Make the resync SKIP bam_dmux_pc_ack() (leaving
     pm_restart() in place) and count a2_power.c:2949 per resync.  If Reading A
     is right and the ACK is the trigger, the signature disappears.  Risk: the
     modem never receives its ACK, so it may hang in a different way -- this
     variant is a probe, not a candidate fix, and must be reverted after.

  D3 (behavioural, the other half).  Skip bam_dmux_pm_restart() instead, keeping
     the ACK.  Together D2 and D3 split Reading A into its two sub-cases.

SEQUENCING DECISION (recorded so it is not re-litigated)
--------------------------------------------------------
The s8.13 coredump-off window is still open (2 of 20 post-disable fatals scored,
bar = 0 AP reboots).  D2/D3 must NOT be deployed inside it: they change which
fatals occur, and a reboot after the swap could not be attributed.  BUT the
window is free to leave running, and it produces fatals passively -- so the
passive half of this test costs nothing and is pre-registered below.

PRE-REGISTRATION (passive, on the running window)
-------------------------------------------------
  P1. The next a2_power.c:2949 fatal in this boot will be preceded by a
      "lost edge" resync within 100 ms.   (Currently 3/3; this tests for 4/4.)
  P2. The next 4 resyncs that are NOT followed by an a2_power.c:2949 within
      100 ms will be counted, to keep the 3/8 sufficiency figure honest.
  FALSIFIER: an a2_power.c:2949 with no resync in the preceding 100 ms breaks
  the association and Reading A/B/C all lose their AP-side handle.

5.3  `pc_timeout_count` IS THE WRONG INSTRUMENT FOR READING C -- DEFINITION READ
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This was recorded in s8.15 as "the cheapest next step".  Done, and the answer is
negative, which is itself worth writing down.

`pc_timeout_count` is incremented in exactly two places, both inside
`bam_dmux_runtime_resume()` (patch 808):

    ret = wait_for_completion_timeout(&dmux->pc_ack_completion,
                                      msecs_to_jiffies(250));
    if (!ret) { atomic64_inc(&dmux->pc_timeout_count);
                dev_warn(dev, "bam_dmux: modem pc-ack timeout during resume\n"); }

    if (!READ_ONCE(dmux->pc_state)) {
        ret = wait_event_timeout(dmux->pc_wait, READ_ONCE(dmux->pc_state),
                                 msecs_to_jiffies(1000));
        if (!ret || !READ_ONCE(dmux->pc_state)) {
            atomic64_inc(&dmux->pc_timeout_count);
            dev_warn(dev, "bam_dmux: modem pc_state wait timeout during resume\n"); } }

  => it counts the AP waiting for the MODEM (250 ms for the vote ACK, then
     1000 ms for pc_state).  Reading C was "the MODEM waits for the AP's ACK and
     times out".  This counter measures the MIRROR of that, so it cannot
     discriminate C.  **Do not design around it.**

  It does bound the AP's side, though: the AP gives the modem 250 ms to ACK a
  vote and 1000 ms for pc_state to rise.  Both are generous next to the 74-86 ms
  the fatal follows the resync by.

  AND THE COUNTER AGREES WITH THE LOG, which is a small but real cross-check:
  `pc_timeout_count` read 4 when exactly four such lines existed in dmesg, and 6
  when six did.  (Contrast the ledger, which is a "noticed" time and lags 0-10 s.)

      reinit_completion(&dmux->pc_ack_completion);          <-- :1421
      mutex_lock(&state_lock); bam_dmux_pc_vote(dmux, true); mutex_unlock();
      ret = wait_for_completion_timeout(&dmux->pc_ack_completion,

        :178 and `bam_dmux_runtime_resume()` :1421 -- or
    (ii) the coupling runs through the MODEM's ACK timing, not the completion
        object at all.

  the pc_ack IRQ.  That is what D1 should now be (see 8.15), NOT the original

7. CORRECTION TO PART 6 -- THE `pm_suspend` DEFICIT IS ACCOUNTING, NOT A DEFECT

Part 6 recorded the `pm_suspend_attempts - pm_suspend_completions` deficit
(7 -> 10) as "recorded, not explained".  Reading the definitions closes it:
  - `pm_suspend_attempts` is incremented in `bam_dmux_runtime_suspend()` after the
    three guards, immediately before `bam_dmux_pc_vote(dmux, false)` (:1389).
  - `pm_suspend_completions` is incremented in exactly TWO places, both requiring
    `pm_suspend_start_ns` to still be non-zero: the pc_irq deassert branch (:1326)
    and `bam_dmux_pc_ack_irq()` (:1354).
  - `pm_suspend_start_ns` is CLEARED by `bam_dmux_runtime_resume()` (:1417) and by
    the RESYNC handler (:797).
  So a suspend attempt that is pre-empted by a resume or by a resync before its
  ACK lands can never increment the completion counter.  **The deficit is the
  count of suspend/resume (and suspend/resync) races -- 11 of 623 = 1.8 % -- and
  its growth is a churn proxy, not evidence of a lost ACK.**  Withdrawn as an
  anomaly.
