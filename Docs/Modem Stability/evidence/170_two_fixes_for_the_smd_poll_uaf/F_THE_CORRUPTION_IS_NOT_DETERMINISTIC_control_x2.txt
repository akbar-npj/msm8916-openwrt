=== WHAT THIS FALSIFIES, AND WHAT IT LEAVES STANDING ===

FALSIFIED: Doc 169 section 5's claim that the defect is DETERMINISTIC ("the teardown
never yields ... so the poller always loses").  It does not always lose.  Two clean
controls, the second with the precondition explicitly forced, both show the poller
WINNING.

WHAT REPLACES IT -- and the measurement is in the log above.  The poller is woken by
wwan_remove_port() at 129.213099 and the channel is freed before 129.215173.  That is
a window of at most 2.07 ms, during which the woken poller must reach poll_freewait()
and unlink.  On a 4-CPU system it usually can.  So this is a RACE with a ~2 ms window,
not a guaranteed loss.

That also re-reads Doc 169's two positive observations correctly: both were taken with
the ms-period sampler (hp3.sh) running, i.e. under heavy load, which is exactly the
condition that would delay the woken poller past the 2 ms window.  That is a HYPOTHESIS
and is directly testable (run the same `echo stop` with hp3.sh armed); it was not tested
here because the device left the USB bus before the experiment could be run.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE FIX: this is the important one, and it is why the control was run
first.  Doc 169 section 8 / Doc 170 section 7 pre-registered "n = 5, predict 0/0" and
called the control "2/2".  A control that is 2/2 in the corpus but 0/2 here cannot
support that pre-registration: five clean runs after deploying a fix would be
indistinguishable from five clean runs WITHOUT it.  The control rate must be measured
before the fix can be scored, and the load hypothesis above is the most likely way to
get a reliable forcing function.

NOT AFFECTED: the arithmetic identification of the corrupted object (Doc 169 section 4,
re-derived from the compiled code in Doc 170 section 3), the poll chain, and the A/B
experiment with qmi-proxy SIGSTOPped (which removed the corruption 1/1 -- a single
observation, now also weaker than it looked).
