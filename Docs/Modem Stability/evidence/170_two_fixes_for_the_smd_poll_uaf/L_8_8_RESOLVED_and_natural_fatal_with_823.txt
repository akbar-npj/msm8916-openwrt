2. WHY THIS CHANGES THE CONCLUSION -- THREE SEPARATE POINTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) **`timeout waiting for ssctl service` is the NORMAL signature of a graceful
    `rproc_stop()` on this device, not an anomaly.** It is emitted by
    `sysmon_stop()` (`qcom_sysmon.c:555`) after a `HZ/2` = 500 ms wait, and it is
    reached only when `crashed == false`.  It appeared here on an ordinary
    `echo stop` that then recovered cleanly.  (It appeared on only ONE of the
    three stops -- the wait returns immediately once `ssctl_comp` has been
    completed by a `sysmon_start`, which is why it is not on all of them.  **Do
    not use its presence or absence to count graceful stops.**)

(b) **`modem pc-ack timeout during resume` also occurs normally and normally
    SELF-HEALS.**  It fired here at 381.900 s and the very next lines are the
    recovery, driven by patch 814's RX watchdog:

        382.913  modem pc_state wait timeout during resume
        382.913  channels not initialized after resume
        383.027  RX watchdog: modem awake (pc line asserted) but no channels, rebuilding
        (then: SSR powerup: modem pc_state=1 (waited 200 ms) -> data works, ping 3/3)

    So the exact line that was the last thing §8.8 printed before its console
    stopped is a line this device **prints and survives**.  What failed in §8.8
    was not the pc-ack timeout itself but the recovery that normally follows it.

(c) **Therefore the §8.8 hang is the pre-existing intermittent AP hang on the SSR
    path** -- the same failure Doc 167 quantified at **~1 in 20-40 SSRs (3-5 %)**,
    which Doc 169/170 had located at the *teardown* and Doc 159 at a *later* site.
    A rare race landing on that boot is a far more parsimonious explanation than a
    regression introduced by an 18-line change that cannot call `rproc_shutdown`,
    especially when the same change ran the same sequence three more times and
    survived all three.

**Revised verdict: 823 is SUBSTANTIALLY EXONERATED, not absolutely.** The
observation is still 1 hang in 4 graceful stops on this boot plus the 1 on the
previous boot; "substantially" rather than "absolutely" because the §8.8 hang was
on a *graceful* stop and the self-heal here was on a *fatal* recovery.  A
controlled boot with the unpatched module (`bash scratch/deploy823.sh --revert`)
remains the clean discriminator and is still **not run**.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE TRAP THIS CREATED, AND THE LESSON
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
`timeout waiting for ssctl service` and `modem pc-ack timeout during resume` were
read in H_first_823_boot_anomaly.txt as markers of an abnormal event, and the whole
"823 is not exculpated" verdict rested on that reading.  Both are in fact **normal
lines on this device** -- the first for any graceful stop, the second whenever the
modem is slow to ACK a power-collapse resume, which the RX watchdog then repairs.

**The generalisable lesson: before treating a kernel message as a fault marker,
establish its BASE RATE -- does it appear on known-good runs?** The two lines were
treated as diagnostic precisely because they were rare in the corpus, and the
corpus is dominated by boots that died before reaching them.  That is the same
error class as Doc 168 §5's "a corpus that structurally cannot contain a line is
not evidence the line's cause is new" -- here it is the mirror image: **a line that
is rare in the corpus may simply be rare in the corpus, not rare in the kernel.**
The cheap fix is one `dmesg | grep -n` on a healthy boot, which is what produced
the table in section 1 above.
