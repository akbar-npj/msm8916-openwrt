=== WHY THIS IS NOT A MEASUREMENT ARTEFACT ===

The site is `lte_ml1_common_timer.c:390` — the tight-clock signature (Doc 162: n=11,
mean 902.353 s, spread 0.508 s), NOT the activity-correlated `a2_power.c:1189`
(mean 695.068 s, spread 872.764 s). So the epoch did not merely die "somewhere"; it
died at the exact site the clock predicts.

=== BONUS: an INDEPENDENT confirmation that the clock is AP/AON-ANCHORED ===

Two consecutive fatals in this AP boot:

  #1  AP 1023.608588   a2_power.c:1189          (the LATE-firing site)
  #2  AP 1926.966036   lte_ml1_common_timer.c:390

  fatal-to-fatal AP interval = 1926.966036 - 1023.608588 = 903.357448 s

Doc 177/179's free-running clock is P = 903.675206 s (AP wall time). Subtract one period
from #2:

  predicted beat          = 1926.966036 - 903.675206 = 1023.290830 s
  #1 residual vs the beat = 1023.608588 - 1023.290830 = +0.317758 s   (a2_power is the
                          late site; Doc 179 residuals for a2_power were +0.788/+3.735/+62.520)
  #2 residual             = 0.000000 s (exact)
  boot phase              = 1023.290830 - 903.675206 = 119.615624 s

  => beats at 119.615624 + k * 903.675206  reproduce BOTH fatals.

*** THE DISCRIMINATOR *** — the modem restarted at AP 1024.311654, i.e. the beat that
produced fatal #2 (1023.290830) was scheduled **1.021 s BEFORE this modem epoch existed**.
A modem-anchored counter cannot have a deadline that precedes its own boot. Therefore the
time base is the AP always-on (AON/XO/RPM) domain — a fresh, independent confirmation of
Doc 177 §3.1, obtained here from a MODEM restart (not an AP reboot).

Note the beat at 119.615624 s was survived; the modem did not assert at the first beat after
its boot. So the condition is not "assert at the first beat"; it is an accumulated one.
