**The convention matters and mixing them is a known trap** (corpus retraction #1 was
exactly a period figure off by one layer).  The corpus states the period as
**~902.3 s of MODEM uptime**; the AP fatal-to-fatal interval is that plus the SSR
downtime.  Here fatal #1 -> #2 is 902.187 s AP but **900.811 s of modem uptime**.

--- what this shows ---

1. PATCH 812 DOES NOT PREVENT THE FATAL.  Three fatals in 1405 s with the fix
   deployed.  As Doc 156 §8 scopes: the fatal is a modem-internal timer and
   patch 812 only stops the AP destroying its own deferred packets.  Patch 812
   is NOT a stability fix.

2. #2 IS THE KNOWN DETERMINISTIC IDLE TIMER.  900.811166 s of modem uptime,
   with signature lte_ml1_sleepmgr_stm.c:4054.  The corpus' own sleepmgr sample
   is 900.864150 s of modem uptime (Doc 149, table row 8) -- the two agree to
   **0.053 s**, on the same signature.  That interval was effectively idle: the
   soak's "burst" generator was still broken (Doc 155 §8.5), so only background
   AP traffic woke the modem.

3. THE INTERVAL COLLAPSES UNDER REAL TRAFFIC.  #3 came only 77.469 s of modem
   uptime after #2, and the corrected harness had restarted with genuine
   40-packet bursts 4.6 s before it.  This is the corpus' "traffic SUPPRESSES
   the deterministic idle fatal and substitutes a variable one"
   (project_fatal_periodicity_and_signatures.md), observed directly.  Fatal #1
   (412.665 s, also under the broken generator) is likewise not the idle period.

4. THE SIGNATURE CHANGES BETWEEN FATALS IN THE SAME BOOT.  #1 and #3 are
   a2_power.c:1189, #2 is lte_ml1_sleepmgr_stm.c:4054.  Confirms the standing
   rule: never classify a fatal by its file:line string -- the descriptor is a
   single mutable global "last fatal" record.

5. RECOVERY IS CLEAN EVERY TIME.  All three ran the full SSR path and the modem
   came back: `port failed halt` (AXI not idle within HALT_ACK_TIMEOUT_US)
   followed by `MBA booted ... loading mpss`, then `is now up` 0.56-0.62 s later
   (fatal#1: 425.385456 -> 425.949302 = 0.564 s; #2: 0.570 s; #3: 0.747 s).
   Doc 154 §6's hang did NOT recur in 3/3 -- further evidence that hang is
   intermittent, not a consequence of the failed halt (Doc 156 §8.1 item 3).

6. NO PACKET LOSS ACROSS ANY OF IT.  In every sample tx_defer_queued ==
   tx_defer_submitted and tx_defer_wiped_live == 0, including the samples
   spanning each SSR.  The fix holds through fatal + SSR.
