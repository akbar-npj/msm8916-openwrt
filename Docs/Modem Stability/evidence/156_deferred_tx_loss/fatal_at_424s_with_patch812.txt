--- what this establishes ---

1. THE FIX DOES NOT PREVENT THE FATAL. Expected and documented (Doc 156 §8) —
   the fatal is a modem-internal timer; patch 812 only stops the AP destroying
   its own deferred packets. The signature is the familiar `a2_power.c:1189`.
   Do not cite patch 812 as a stability fix.

2. THE TIMING IS VARIABLE, again. 424.57 s AP, and the modem had booted at
   ~11.2 s, so ~413 s of modem uptime. This is neither the ~902 s idle
   deterministic timer nor the ~900 s family; it is consistent with the corpus'
   "`a2_power.c:1189` is a RATE, not a period" (172 / 706 / 918 / 1824 / 2729 s
   previously observed) and with "traffic suppresses the deterministic idle
   fatal and substitutes a variable one" (Doc 149 §3). The traffic here was
   low-rate and bursty (one DNS query per 30 s), which is neither the idle case
   nor the sustained case.

3. THE MODEM RESTARTED SUCCESSFULLY THIS TIME — and `port failed halt`
   appeared anyway. Doc 154 §6 recorded a fatal after which the modem never
   came back: `port failed halt`, then a stall at `MBA booted without debug
   policy, loading mpss` with nothing after it, `state = offline`, reboot
   required. The identical `port failed halt` warning (425.340321) is present
   here and the very next line is the same `MBA booted ... loading mpss`
   (425.385456) — and this time the load completed in ~0.56 s
   (`remote processor ... is now up` at 425.949302).
   => `port failed halt` (the AXI port not idle within HALT_ACK_TIMEOUT_US,
   `q6v5proc_halt_axi_port` `:974`) is a WARNING and is NOT sufficient to
   cause the hang. Doc 154 §6.1's "the SCM ownership transfer blocks while the
   AXI port is stuck" remains a hypothesis, but the hang is INTERMITTENT, not
   a deterministic consequence of the failed halt.

4. Recovery is clean: the DNS-after-idle test's remaining rounds all
   succeeded, `tx_defer_wiped_live` stayed 0, and `defer_queued ==
   defer_submitted` across the SSR.
